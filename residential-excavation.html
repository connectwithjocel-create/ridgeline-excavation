Overview

What residential excavation involves.

For homeowners and custom home builders, residential sitework pulls several services together — access, clearing, foundation excavation, utilities and final grade — around one house and one lot.

Every property is different. The right approach depends on the plans, the ground, access and any engineering or permit requirements — which is why the work starts with understanding the site.

Sitework considerations

What shapes the work.

Neighbors & access

Residential streets and shared drives limit equipment and staging.

Existing landscaping

Protecting what the owner wants to keep.

Sequencing

Excavation fits between surveyors, foundation crews and utilities.

Final grade

The last pass determines how water treats the home for years.

Typical applications

Where it applies.

Common situations where residential excavation is part of the project.

APP-01

Custom home sites

From first access to final grade around a new home.

APP-02

Hillside & walkout lots

Sloped lots common along the Front Range that need careful cut and fill.

APP-03

Acreage properties

Driveways, pads and drainage on rural parcels.

APP-04

Additions & garages

Excavation that works around an existing home and yard.

Planning requirements

Before the first cut.

Having these in hand keeps the project moving and helps produce a more accurate estimate.

  • Site plan and foundation plans
  • Builder schedule and trade sequencing
  • HOA or covenant requirements
  • Utility locates and permits

FAQ

Residential questions.

Request an Estimate
Can you work with my builder?

Yes. Residential sitework is usually coordinated with the builder's schedule and other trades.

I'm buying land — can you look at it before I build?

A site evaluation can help you understand access, slope and visible conditions. It doesn't replace engineering, soils testing or surveys.

What affects residential excavation cost?

Site access, soil and rock, slope, foundation type, haul-off, and the overall scope all play a role. Estimates follow a review of plans and the site.
